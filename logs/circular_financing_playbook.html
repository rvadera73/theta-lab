—

Two corrections carried through this revision

No long options, ever. Earlier drafts of this memo suggested long put debit spreads on ORCL/CRWV. That's not how this book trades — every structure below is short-premium only. ORCL is dropped from the plan entirely (too binary/already-moved to touch in either direction), not just re-structured.
No calls, in a BULL regime. Short call credit spreads were suggested as the "hedge" substitute — also wrong. This book's own data shows stagger call legs have underperformed put legs, and a persistent bull regime structurally punishes being short calls (the trend grinds through strikes). Every position in this plan, aggressive or defensive, is a cash-secured short put. Risk management happens entirely through name selection, delta, size, and DTE — not through an offsetting structure.

02

The domino loop — why the order is 2000, then 2007

One structural fragility, two possible entry doors, in a specific mechanical order.

The credit/leverage layer is built on top of today's elevated equity valuations — so the valuation layer has to crack first before the credit layer can feel it.

Phase 1 · Ignition

A valuation/sentiment crack in the narrow, richly-valued leadership. Trigger candidates: the Anthropic IPO pricing weak, or a hyperscaler capex-guidance disappointment in Q3 earnings.

↓
Phase 2 · Credit exposure

Falling equity values impair the collateral behind circular-financing debt. CoreWeave/NBIS-type leverage gets marked down; private-credit funds face NAV markdowns and redemption requests.

↓
Phase 2 · Contagion

Funds gate redemptions or fire-sell the loans — the actual 2007 pattern, named directly by FSOC/FSB.

Loops back: fire-sale prices make the next round of capex funding costlier, cutting the growth assumptions behind Nvidia's "cheap" forward multiple — compressing valuations further and reopening Phase 1.

The 2006→2008 precedent sets the timing lag

Phase 2 is not a 90-day risk even if Phase 1 fires — the entire basis for gating Phase-2-style caution below rather than treating it as concurrent.
